# Codebook policy v1

A separate pass beside codebook v3.4, with its own questions and output. It records who sets the policy of each decision-maker and how many parties' interests the decision-makers serve. Answer only the questions below; this codebook has no other fields.

Code each paper from its title and abstract only, reading the abstract to its end. Code the setting the paper's method or analysis is meant for (its intended use or real-world referent), even when the paper's own experiments simulate that setting; do not code settings mentioned only in passing (an example task, a motivation sentence). A decision-maker is any learner, model, agent, player, person or organization that chooses actions in that setting. A paper in which one developer trains or runs one model is the default setting: policy_setters one, principals one, partners_from_others no. Use na only where the abstract does not fix the answer, or where the paper has no decision-maker at all (for example a pure hypothesis test or a dataset with no learner).

POLICY_SETTERS: in the setting the method or analysis is meant for, who sets the policy of each decision-maker, that is, who trains, programs, prompts or otherwise decides how it acts? one: one party sets every decision-maker's policy. This includes a single model or learner; a team or population of agents that one developer trains; multi-agent learning in which the authors train every agent, whether or not each agent has its own reward; a simulated market or society whose every agent the designer programs; and players whose strategies the paper's own algorithm computes or learns as one system (an equilibrium solver used as an analysis tool). several: at least two decision-makers have policies set by different parties, each setting its own: agents built, trained or deployed separately by different parties, or people or organizations who choose their own actions; this includes formal-model players that the abstract presents as choosing for themselves in the setting the analysis is meant for. na: the abstract does not say who sets the decision-makers' policies, or there is no decision-maker. Decide by who sets each policy, never by the decision-makers' roles, names or interests, and never by who designs their rewards, incentives or the rules they act under: a party that designs the rewards or the rules but leaves each decision-maker to choose its own actions does not set their policies.

PRINCIPALS: how many distinct parties' interests do the decision-makers serve in that setting? one: all decision-makers serve one party: one user, one organization, or one team objective. A team trained by one developer for one shared reward serves one principal, and so do agents with separate rewards that one developer designs to serve its own goal. several: the decision-makers serve at least two distinct parties, whether those parties' interests are aligned or conflicting. na: the abstract does not say whose interests the decision-makers serve, or there is no decision-maker. Count parties, not agents: many agents serving one party are one principal.

PARTNERS_FROM_OTHERS: yes if the method is built to act with or against agents that other parties train or run and that it has not seen in training; no otherwise; na if there is no decision-maker.

Output one JSON object per paper with exactly these keys: "id", "policy_setters", "policy_quote", "principals", "principals_quote", "partners_from_others". "policy_quote" is the shortest verbatim phrase from the title or abstract that decided POLICY_SETTERS, and "principals_quote" the shortest verbatim phrase that decided PRINCIPALS; each is an empty string if no phrase decided it.

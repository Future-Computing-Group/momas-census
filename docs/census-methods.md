# Census and deployment coding: further analyses

This document carries method details and analyses of the census and the deployment coding that the Supplementary Information (SI) does not report. It types no number. Every number it refers to is a generated fragment: `si/NAME.tex` (fragments the SI also prints) or `docs/fragments/NAME.tex` (fragments only this document cites), written by `python3 si_tables.py` from the coded data and regenerated by `test_clean_clone.sh`. A fragment named in brackets, such as [tab-delineation], is that file. Shares are percentages; ranges in parentheses are conservative 95% bounds unless stated otherwise. Tables are LaTeX rows, one row per line, columns separated by `&`.

## 1. Delineation of the frame

Accepted papers of NeurIPS and ICML (2020 and 2025) came from the conferences' public lists of accepted papers, which include titles, author lists with institutions and abstracts: `https://neurips.cc/static/virtual/data/neurips-2020-orals-posters.json`, with the same path for NeurIPS 2025 and for ICML (for example `https://icml.cc/static/virtual/data/icml-2025-orals-posters.json`), retrieved on 2026-11-06. ICLR 2025 used the same kind of list (`https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2025-orals-posters.json`), retrieved on 2026-11-07. Oral presentations listed more than once were deduplicated by normalized title. ACM EC 2025 papers came from the OpenAlex works API with the filter `primary_location.source.id:S4363608973,publication_year:2025`, retrieved on 2026-11-06, with abstracts rebuilt from the inverted index; every record was kept, including research papers that OpenAlex types as paratext. AAMAS 2025 papers are the Research Paper Track of the IFAAMAS table of contents, with the publisher's Crossref abstracts (`fetch_frames.py`).

The 2025 main tracks are identified by each record's OpenReview group. A paper counts as main track if any of its listings carries the main-track group, since NeurIPS 2025 lists its Mexico City orals first under a satellite group. The Datasets and Benchmarks, position-paper and journal-track records of 2025 [n-nonmain], which the 2020 lists do not contain, were coded but enter no estimate. The sha256 of every archived list is in `data/raw-manifest.json`; `fetch_frames.py` refetches the lists and checks them against it. The released `data/frames-delineation.json` keeps, of each archived list, only the fields the frame table reads (title, OpenReview group, OpenAlex type). Coders saw only a paper's title and abstract, under an opaque identifier and without venue or year.

The frame table [tab-delineation] gives, per conference and year: the raw records in the archived list, the duplicate listings removed, the papers coded, the papers in the main track, and the rule that identifies the main track with the records outside it (journal track: records whose listing points to a journal rather than an OpenReview group).

## 2. Codebook details (census, version 3.4)

The full codebook is `codebook-v3.md`. Details that the SI abbreviates: under AGENT, learners that act over rounds are agents, while one-shot participants in a mechanism and optimisation methods are not. Under INTERESTS, several aligned includes a cooperating team that one developer builds. The first OWNERS step (several) also covers applicants and voters; the second (one) also covers equilibrium computation used as an analysis tool and self-play that produces one policy. The coder answers the OWNERS steps as separate questions, and OWNERS is derived from the answers. FOCUS interaction also covers fairness across parties. LLM_BASIS counts vision-language models among the foundation models that can choose the agents' actions.

EVAL_RUN codes the experiments separately from the setting: one when the authors run every decision-maker, including several vendors' models run side by side, and several when decision-makers run by independent parties take part. CROSS_VENDOR records whether models from different developers interact in a paper's experiments. Neither EVAL_RUN nor CROSS_VENDOR enters the shares. The OWNERS rule and its decomposition into separate questions were fixed after pilot runs of the first-stage model, before the full run. The cells of the Perspective come from the policy codebook (`codebook-policy-v2.2.md`), described in SI Note 1.

## 3. The two-stage estimator: further details

Stage 1 ran the instruction-tuned Gemma-4-31B with quantization-aware 4-bit weights on one GPU of a university cluster at temperature 0, with the codebook as system prompt and outputs constrained to a JSON schema (`roihu/xfam.sbatch`, `data/xfam/PROVENANCE.md`). Besides the random slices among agent and other papers, stage 2 drew further slice papers among the 2025 LLM-agent papers and the other papers to narrow the bounds. A stratum with too few slice papers takes the pooled rate of its slice (`twostage.py`).

Precision of the first stage [screen-precision] and its misses in the random slices, by stratum [screen-misses]. The EC strata are thin, but EC is counted in full. Because only the screen-positive papers go to confirmation in full, the simulated share rests mostly on the slices. The set of papers both stages coded holds every screen-positive paper, so it is enriched for the hard cases, and the agreement of the two stages on it [tab-screen-agreement] is not that of a random sample.

## 4. The cell by interests, and adversarial settings

The cell with several principals and several owners split by the version 3.4 INTERESTS code, with adversarial settings added, estimated as in the SI estimates table [tab-decomp]. Columns: population; the cell; aligned; conflicting; adversarial; the cell and the adversarial settings together. Aligned and conflicting add up to the cell. The short form the SI prints is [decomp-short].

Even where a paper assumes several owners, the owners can share one objective: a 2025 framework motivated by "privacy-preserving collaboration across organizations" removes the central orchestrator (Y. Yang et al., "AgentNet: Decentralized Evolutionary Coordination for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems", NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2504.00587), and its organizations differ only in what they know.

## 5. Differences between years and ratios: five interval methods

The conservative bounds are bounds per population, not intervals for a difference. The table [tab-contrasts] gives the 2025 minus 2020 differences, and the ratio of the simulated share to the share with several principals and several owners, with five 95% intervals, all holding the confirmed counts fixed: the differences or ratios of the conservative bounds; a bootstrap that resamples the slice papers within their strata (2,000 draws); the same bootstrap that also resamples each population's papers, treating the census as one draw from what the venues publish; draws of each stratum's rate from its Jeffreys posterior, which lets a stratum whose slice holds no paper of the cell contribute misses; and the Jeffreys draws with the papers resampled. Columns: contrast; point; conservative; slices; papers; Jeffreys; Jeffreys with papers. The Jeffreys draws with papers resampled are quoted for differences [trend-several, trend-interaction, trend-simulated-agent]; for ratios, whose two terms share the slice papers, the SI quotes the conservative bounds [ratio-agent, ratio-llm-other]. The tipping point of the ratio is [tip-ratio].

## 8. The last author's sector

Author institutions from the conference lists were classified as industry or academic by name rules (`affil.py`). Because author order is sometimes alphabetical, which makes position meaningless, the comparison uses the last author's sector in main-track papers of 2020 and 2025 with at least two authors listed in non-alphabetical order, and estimates the share with several principals and several owners in each group with the two-stage estimator. Last authors whose institution could not be classified: [sector-unclassified]. Sensitivities: an institution string that names both a company and an academic institution counts as industry (primary), as academic, or is dropped; unclassified last authors are dropped (primary), counted as academic, or counted as industry. The interval of the difference comes from Jeffreys draws of the stratum rates with the papers resampled.

The primary comparison is [sector-short] and every variant is in [tab-sector] (columns: variant; industry papers; industry share with bounds; academic papers; academic share with bounds; industry minus academic difference in points with its 95% interval). The comparison measures which settings papers address, not whether research is captured by those who fund it. Its sensitivity to coder error is [tip-sector].

## 10. The earlier coding stage (codebook version 2)

Before the census, Claude Sonnet 5.5 coders coded a stratified sample of abstracts [n-coded] under codebook version 2 (`codebook.md`), whose OWNERS field did not separate the setting from the experiments; its shares enter no estimate. The checks below belong to that stage and to codebook version 2, not to the census codes.

Composition of the coded sample [tab-sample] (columns: venue and year; coded papers; random; agent keyword; LLM-agent keyword; control); a paper in several strata is counted in each column but coded once.

A random fifth of the coded papers [n-overlap] was coded a second time by an independent coder working from the same codebook. In some early batches coders saw only the opening of long abstracts; those batches were re-coded from the full abstracts, and the re-coded labels are the ones used [reread]. Inter-coder agreement on the double-coded papers [tab-agreement] (columns: field; agreement; Cohen's kappa overall, for abstracts longer and shorter than 900 characters).

The author coded a sample blind [n-pi], drawn from the 2025 keyword strata, the random strata and EC, and then saw the coders' reading and justification for every field on which they differed [pi-recon]. The author's blind codes against the coders' codes are [tab-pi] (columns: field; agreement; kappa).

## 11. Codebook versions

Census: version 0 (pilot) had one PRINCIPALS field; version 1 split it into INTERESTS and OWNERS; version 2 (`codebook.md`, the earlier coding stage) set the default for one developer's model. Version 2 received one clarification after the author's check: when the developer generates the other parties (by simulation, self-play, or one model that plays every side), OWNERS is one; when the method is analysed against parties who choose their own responses, OWNERS is several. Version 3 (`codebook-v3.md`) coded the setting separately from the experiments (EVAL_RUN), added CROSS_VENDOR and folded in LLM_BASIS (from `codebook-llm.md`, version 2); its revisions after smoke runs added FOCUS (3.2), the ordered OWNERS decision (3.3) and the separate OWNERS questions (3.4, the version used for the census). The policy pass used `codebook-policy.md` (version 1) and then `codebook-policy-v2.md`, `codebook-policy-v2.1.md` and `codebook-policy-v2.2.md` (the version of the census cells; its changelog lists the rules R5 to R10 that the pilot's rule-implied codes in `data/pilot/` refer to). The parties split uses `codebook-parties.md` (version 1).

Deployments: version 2 (`codebook-deploy.md`) was revised once after a pilot coded by two independent coders; version 3 (`codebook-deploy-roleindep.md`) changes REACH only, deciding cross-owner by the system the agent acts on rather than by the kind of other party; version 4 (`codebook-deploy-layers.md`) codes every layer another party operates (SI Note 2).

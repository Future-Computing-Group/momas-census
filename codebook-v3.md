# Codebook v3.4

v3 (2026-11-06, after a check of the coding): v2's fields and conventions are kept, and three fields are added. The check showed that OWNERS mixed two things: the setting a paper's method is meant for, and the way the paper's own experiments are run (one laboratory often runs every agent, including models from several vendors). v3 codes them separately: OWNERS is now explicitly the setting, EVAL_RUN is the experiments, and CROSS_VENDOR records whether models from different developers interact. LLM_BASIS (codebook-llm.md v2) is folded in so one pass codes everything. v3.1 (after a 100-paper smoke run): worked examples for OWNERS, the one-shot and optimiser rules for AGENT, the team rule for INTERESTS, a narrower na, and an empty quote when nothing decides OWNERS. v3.2 (after a second smoke run and the author's coding decision): FOCUS added; the one-shot rule narrowed so that learners acting over rounds stay agents; a method one developer trains or runs is the default setting, never na; game-playing agents built to face other parties are several owners in their setting; side-by-side comparisons are not cross-vendor interaction. v3.3 (after a third smoke run, where a list of examples made OWNERS unstable): OWNERS is decided by an ordered rule, and FOCUS for the default setting is agent. v3.4 (after a fourth smoke run): the OWNERS steps are also answered as three separate questions, from which OWNERS and FOCUS na are derived.

Code each paper from its title and abstract only, reading the abstract to its end. Code what the paper's method or analysis assumes, not settings mentioned only in passing (an example task, a motivation sentence). A paper in which one developer trains or runs one model is the default setting: interests one, owners one, control central, boundary no, eval_run one. Use na only where the abstract does not fix the field, or where the paper has no learner, decision-maker or analyst (for example a pure hypothesis test).

AGENT: yes if the paper's subject is one or more decision-makers that act over several steps in an environment (reinforcement-learning agents, language-model agents that call tools or act on the web or in software, robots, bidders, players); no if an agent appears only as a component used to build a dataset or as a one-shot language-model call. Participants who make one single decision in a one-shot mechanism, contract, auction or voting rule are not agents, and an optimisation or numerical method (a solver, an optimiser, a sampler) is not an agent. Learners that act over rounds are agents: bandit learners, reinforcement-learning and Bayesian-optimisation policies, and bidders or sellers in repeated or dynamic mechanisms.

DATA: pooled (one dataset under one controller) / partitioned (several owners hold data they do not pool, e.g. federated or cross-silo) / na.

OBJECTIVE: single (one loss, reward or utility, including a team reward shared by several agents or a min-max game internal to one learner) / multiple (decision-makers have their own, possibly conflicting objectives) / na.

CONTROL: central (one party designs, trains or orchestrates every decision-maker, even if computation or data is distributed; a federated server counts as central) / decentralized (no single party controls every decision-maker) / na.

INTERESTS (whose interests the formal setting models; several cooperating agents with a shared goal are several-aligned, also when one developer builds the whole team, which OWNERS then codes one; a single model or learner is one): one / several-aligned (several parties with identical or shared goals) / several-conflicting (parties with distinct, possibly opposed goals: game opponents, bidders, negotiators, competing firms, applicants facing a classifier) / adversarial (an attacker or threat model against one protected party) / na.

OWNERS (the setting): how many independent parties own or control the decision-makers in the situation the method or analysis is meant for, its intended use or real-world referent, even when the paper's experiments simulate that situation. Decide by these steps, in order, and stop at the first that applies:
(1) The agents are built to face decision-makers that other parties own or that act for themselves: other organizations' or users' agents, human players or participants, bidders, applicants, voters, firms or consumers, independent self-interested agents whose incentives a designer or planner sets, or an external attacker. Code several. A game-playing agent meant to play against humans or other parties' agents is several even when trained by self-play.
(2) Otherwise, one party trains, runs or deploys every decision-maker: a single model or learner, a team of cooperating agents built by one developer, self-play or population training that produces one policy, or a method that computes or learns an equilibrium as an analysis tool. Code one.
(3) Otherwise, the abstract does not say who controls the decision-makers. Code na. Decentralized computation alone is not several owners (see CONTROL).

EVAL_RUN (the experiments): who runs the decision-makers in the paper's own experiments, simulations or analysis. one: the authors run all of them (simulated opponents, self-play, several vendors' models run by the authors, one model that plays every side). several: the experiments involve decision-makers run by independent parties (human participants acting for themselves, field data on independent firms or users, a live deployment across organizations). na: the paper has no experiments or analysis of decision-makers, or the abstract does not say.

CROSS_VENDOR: yes if the experiments let models from different developers (for example a GPT model, a Claude model and a Llama model) interact with one another in the same environment; no if models are only compared side by side on the same task, ranked on a leaderboard or benchmarked one at a time, or if all interacting models come from one developer; na if there are no interacting models.

FOCUS (what the contribution is about): agent if the contribution improves or analyses the performance, decisions or internals of one agent or one team, treating the other parties as part of its environment (an agent that wins more games, negotiates better, plays mixed-motive games better, or best-responds to others); interaction if the contribution concerns the joint outcome or the rules between the parties (efficiency, welfare, fairness across parties, equilibrium properties, collusion, incentive compatibility, the design of a mechanism, market, protocol or institution, or the governance of the exchange); na only if the paper has no learner, model, decision-maker or analyst at all. The default setting of one developer training or running one model is agent, never na. When a paper studies learning agents and its analysis is about the outcome they reach together (the equilibrium, the prices, the collusion), the focus is interaction; when it is about improving one agent's payoff, the focus is agent.

BOUNDARY: yes if decision-makers or data owners on different sides of an ownership line interact in the setting; no otherwise.

LLM_BASIS (code only when AGENT = yes; otherwise na): yes if the decision-makers the paper studies are large language models or are driven by one (a language model, vision-language model or multimodal foundation model chooses the actions, possibly through tools, code or a scaffold), including agents the title or abstract calls LLM-, VLM- or MLLM-based and "reasoning models" acting as agents; no if the agents are of another kind (reinforcement-learning policies not built on a language model, bandit learners, classical planners, robots with conventional controllers, game-theoretic players, human participants, in-context RL agents or diffusion planners without a language model), including papers that use a language model only as a tool, reward model, data generator or evaluator for agents that are not themselves language models; na if the abstract does not say what the agents are built on (words such as "agentic", "web agent" or "GUI agent" are not enough by themselves).

DECOMPOSITION: answer three questions before OWNERS. "has_decision_maker": yes if the paper has any learner, model, agent, decision-maker or analyst (the default setting is yes); no otherwise. "faces_other_parties": yes if OWNERS step 1 applies (the decision-makers are built to face, or are, parties of other owners: other parties' agents, humans, bidders, applicants, voters or firms acting for themselves, independent self-interested agents whose incentives a planner sets, or an attacker); no otherwise. "faces_quote": the shortest verbatim phrase from the abstract that shows it, or an empty string. "one_party_runs_all": yes if one party trains, runs or deploys every decision-maker in the setting (OWNERS step 2); no otherwise. OWNERS is derived from these answers (several if faces_other_parties is yes, else one if has_decision_maker is yes, else na), and FOCUS is na exactly when has_decision_maker is no; still give your own OWNERS and FOCUS.

Output one JSON object per paper with exactly these keys: "id", "agent", "data", "objective", "control", "interests", "has_decision_maker", "faces_other_parties", "faces_quote", "one_party_runs_all", "owners", "eval_run", "cross_vendor", "focus", "boundary", "llm_basis", "confidence" (high / medium / low), "quote" (the shortest verbatim phrase from the abstract that decided OWNERS, or an empty string if no phrase decided it). Confidence: high when the abstract states the deciding fact, medium when it implies it, low when you inferred it from the topic alone.
